The Price of a Chair
"Ten lives or this?" a simple test. The chair, of course it's Louis' best...
read →
"Ten lives or this?" a simple test. The chair, of course it's Louis' best. It doesn't rock. It doesn't care. And every day someone picks the chair. They say, "I've earned it. I was smart." (As if the world's a merit chart.) They built their thrones from spines and sweat. Call it fair. And don't regret. One child dies, but stocks arise. A win. A yacht. A suit that lies. "It's not my fault," the wealthy plead. (They sleep just fine. The dead don't need.) They sip champagne from crying eyes. They dine where famine never dies. And every fork they lift insists, "Deserving hands wear diamond wrists." You ask for crumbs? Why not aspire? They toast success while towns catch fire. Their gold is clean washed in denial. And corpses? Just a market file. The poor should rise, they love to say. In boots worn through, and thick with clay. If dreams decay in sallow skin, It's proof of weakness. Proof of sin. What's fairness but a child's scream? They bought the world. Hope's just a dream. The poor should strive, they like to say. (Just not too near. And not their way.) A girl in cobalt mines has no name-- but makes your phone bright. Children fold in factory light, lungs grinding glass, to bind your name and mine. There are hands at the end of every "Made In" tag. Blood. So take your seat. The room is still. The air is sweet. Your glass -- refill. Ignore the names beneath your thighs, they're buried deep. And silence buys. Now -- !
